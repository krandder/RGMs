# Phase 1 control benchmark: protocol v1

Status: fixed before benchmark training or tuning. Implementation smoke tests may diagnose software failures; they do not select hyperparameters or change these criteria. Any substantive change requires a dated amendment retaining this file. This is an engineering benchmark on small, fully observed worlds, not a claim about general intelligence or novelty.

## Decision and success criteria

The question is whether a hierarchy built by the live upstream fast-structure-learning constructor can support useful action selection. We distinguish predictive representation, controllable actions, and planning performance. A custom controller attached to upstream learned levels will be named an adapter; it will not be described as the unmodified upstream variational planner.

For each task, define normalized performance as (agent expected return − uniform-random expected return)/(exact optimal expected return − uniform-random expected return). The main practical gate at 4,096 logged transitions is mean normalized performance at least 0.90 on every task, at least eight of ten training seeds at least 0.80 per task, and mean performance no more than 0.05 below the stronger of flat active inference and tabular Q-learning on any task. Runtime gate: at most 60 wall seconds per task/seed/final-budget fit and policy construction, and at most 0.10 seconds per decision on the measured CPU. Failure remains a result. Means and uncertainty accompany gates; a passed engineering gate is not a statistical superiority claim.

Sample efficiency is the normalized-return curve at budgets 256, 1,024 and 4,096, its trapezoidal area against log2(data budget), and the first tested budget reaching 0.90. Do not interpolate a smaller successful budget. Report fit, policy construction and evaluation wall/CPU times separately. If shared-host contention dominates wall time, report it alongside CPU time without discarding slow runs.

## Tasks and exact reference

All learners receive the same categorical observation factors, primitive action labels and observed rewards. They do not receive the transition kernel. The evaluator alone receives the enumerated kernel for exact finite-horizon dynamic programming and policy evaluation.

1. **Pong-like interception.** Three paddle and ball columns, ball direction ±1, and four steps until interception. Left/stay/right moves the paddle one column with clipping. The ball reflects at edges; with probability 0.10 its direction reverses before moving. At the fourth step the reward is one exactly when paddle and ball columns coincide; otherwise zero. Initial ball column and direction are uniform, paddle starts centrally. Ball position, direction, paddle position and flight phase are observed.
2. **Key and door corridor.** Five positions 0–4, start at 1 without the key, key collected at position 0, goal at 4, and passage from 3 to 4 requires the key. Left/stay/right, clipped at boundaries. Horizon eight. Each nonterminal step costs 0.01, and first arrival at the goal pays one. Goal is absorbing with zero further reward. Position and possession of the key are observed. This tests delayed reward and context-dependent control.
3. **Exogenous weather.** Rain/dry context flips independently of action with probability 0.20. Open/close sets the umbrella. Per-step reward is 1 − rain×(1−umbrella) − 0.30×umbrella, evaluated after the weather transition and action. Horizon eight; initial weather is uniform and umbrella closed. Both factors are observed. This tests the mistake of selecting a favorable exogenous transition as though it were an action.

Enumerate every state/action transition and check probability normalization. Backward dynamic programming gives the optimal value and uniform-random value without sampling error. Independently check these against exhaustive policy enumeration on reduced-horizon instances, explicit shortest-path reasoning for the corridor, and direct one-step conditional calculations for weather. Include the time remaining in finite-horizon policies for every learner; it is not hidden information granted to one method.

## Shared data and uncertainty

Use ten training seeds 101–110 and nested prefixes of one 4,096-transition random-policy log per task/seed. Episode boundaries are stored; no learner may treat reset jumps as controllable transitions. Preserve the logs in a compact public artifact with state factors, action, reward, next state, terminal flag, episode and step. These accessors can also support later hierarchy-posterior experiments.

Evaluation policies are frozen before evaluation and never update from evaluation data. Primary returns come from exact evaluation under the known kernel, so uncertainty is over the ten training datasets. Also run 200 common-random-number episodes per task/seed with evaluation seeds 10,001–10,010 as a simulation cross-check; report the Monte Carlo discrepancy. Use 10,000 paired bootstrap draws over the ten training seeds (bootstrap RNG 20261121) for descriptive 95% intervals on mean return and paired method differences. All seeds count, including failures; record software failures separately and do not silently substitute methods. No hyperparameter search on evaluation runs.

## Methods and fixed controls

- **Random:** uniform primitive actions, analytically evaluated and simulated.
- **Tabular Q-learning:** the same logged transitions, with remaining time in the state, discount one, zero initialization, 20 replay passes, per-state-action learning rate visit_count^−0.6, deterministic smallest-index tie break. Report replay work and time; these are compute, not new environment samples.
- **Flat discrete active inference:** actual `inferactively-pymdp`, with its package version, licence and invoked APIs recorded. Learn action-conditioned categorical transitions and reward-outcome likelihoods from the same log. Use expected-free-energy policy inference with utility preferences proportional to observed task reward, observation/state information gain disabled for the fully observed exploitation test, inverse temperature 16, and a fixed receding horizon up to four. Do not call a home-written greedy reward rule pymdp. If the real package cannot run, report the missing comparison rather than inventing a substitute.
- **Flat learned-model planning:** empirical action-conditioned transition/reward model, unknown state-action rows self-loop with zero reward, full remaining-horizon dynamic programming. This separates model estimation from planner truncation and hierarchy.
- **RGM path adapter:** call the live `python_src.toolbox.DEM.spm_faster_structure_learning.spm_faster_structure_learning` with native grouping, spatial scale one and temporal scale two. Preserve the returned hierarchy and level metadata. Use its learned state/path likelihoods to infer hierarchical context and construct candidate short action sequences from the shared log. The initial adapter scores learned paths as selectable successors, preserving the questionable control assumption for diagnosis. Primitive execution uses the logged conditional action decoder; no direct setting of exogenous state is permitted.
- **Action-grounded RGM adapter:** use the same upstream hierarchy and candidate sequences, but score actual primitive action implementations under the empirical action-conditioned joint transition model. Preserve context–action dependencies and reward dependence on joint states. Replan after each observed primitive step. Upper-level state/path mappings must affect candidate options or decisions; a constructor called only for logging does not qualify.
- **Ablations:** disable upper-level candidate restrictions using the same empirical model; remove action conditioning while retaining the same hierarchy; and remove joint-context reward lifting on the weather task. Report exact decision disagreements and values, not only hierarchy-construction calls. These distinguish the hierarchy’s contribution from the control repair and the reward map.

For hierarchical adapters, use level two when available, a maximum option length of four, and at most 32 distinct logged action sequences per inferred context ordered by frequency then lexicographically. Include all primitive one-step actions as a documented recovery mechanism; record how often one-step recovery is selected. Unseen contexts use the primitive set and an explicit unknown-state flag. Report hierarchy-disabled equality if primitive recovery makes hierarchy behaviorally redundant. Do not claim a hierarchical advantage from equal decisions or from a bookkeeping-only hierarchy.

The FSL constructor receives contiguous observation streams from the shared log. Episode resets must be represented explicitly as boundaries or excluded from all learned control edges; record any reset-boundary contamination of predictive FSL paths as a limitation and test a boundary-aware construction where feasible. Never supply true transition probabilities, optimal actions, privileged factor-control masks or additional trajectories to one learned method.

## Diagnostics and proof obligations

Record constructor source path/hash, upstream commit, all returned level counts/state and path cardinalities, tensor nonzeros/bytes, observed coverage and unknown-context rate. Compression is a storage/representation measurement and is reported separately from returns. A smaller observed state alphabet is not evidence of generalization to unseen combinations. Save full policy tables or deterministic decision accessors and compact trajectories.

Tests must show that the real constructor creates multiple live levels, learned mappings are normalized/decoded consistently, scrambling or removing upper-level mappings changes the adapter’s candidate construction, every selected command is a legal primitive action, weather dynamics remain action-independent in the environment, preferences keep the rain–umbrella interaction when required, and oracle/evaluation calculations agree with independently derived checks. Baseline calls must have direct API witnesses. Dataset and result hashes make every reported number reproducible.

Software fixes may repair shape/indexing/integration failures without changing the protocol. Practical modeling fixes beyond the predeclared adapters are exploratory amendments with their own preserved results. Report negative outcomes, missing comparisons and controls that explain away an apparent gain.

## Literature and scope

Read before this protocol: Zaghw et al., *Renormalising Generative Models for Active Inference: Foundations, Derivations, and Verification* (arXiv:2608.09512), the live GPL-2.0-or-later RGM README and constructor, and Zaghw–Da Costa’s 20 October 2026 note *Limitations of Renormalizing Generative Models as Hierarchical Planning Models*. The note motivates the action-grounding, unseen-state and joint-preference controls. The local checked extraction of Da Costa’s papers (Q2/Q4), the GFlowNet facts note, and the MATS writing stack were also consulted. This benchmark does not claim that its adapters are novel or that it resolves the general planning limitations in that note.

Run on CPU at niceness 19 with one BLAS/OpenMP thread. No cloud, private datasets, external correspondence, upstream issues or upstream pull requests. Preserve GPL terms; check pymdp’s licence before installation. Publish only generic code, synthetic worlds and results. Root handles repository commits, result review and presentation.
